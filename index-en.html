    Atlas of French America

    ✦

    From the Great Lakes to the Gulf of Mexico, from New England to Oregon, French-speaking voyageurs, traders, settlers and workers left their traces across the whole continent. This network of geohistorical atlases maps them, each from its own sources; each entry lives in a single atlas and the others refer to it.

    Archives and news

    Click a point to open its entry. When several entries share the same place (Taos, Mora, Fort El Pueblo…), the full list is shown.

    The atlas brings together the interview notebooks of Francis W. Cragin (1902-1923), surveyed for every mention of French speakers, the point entries of the Aubry Atlas, the traces found in the course of research (literature, web), and a growing archive.org library of public-domain books read in full, from Louisiana to Montana. The reading room has one tab per person from French Fur Traders and Voyageurs in the American West (Hafen, ed.); Maurice LeDuc keeps the sky-blue color.

    Reading pages: The French Canadians of the West (Tassé, 1878) · The mountain men (Hafen) · French Illinois · Étienne Provost.

    English version: the interface is translated; the entries (more than 34,000) are being translated in batches, and those not yet translated are shown in the original French. The testimonies collected by Cragin are pioneers' memories, often recorded late. Each entry gives the page of the notebooks PDF, the level of certainty of the location and, where needed, a critical note.

    About the atlas

    A geohistorical atlas of French speakers (French Canadians, Missouri and Illinois Creoles, French from France, French-speaking Swiss) and of the events that concern them in North America, starting with the American West.

    Sources

    • Cragin — Francis Whittemore Cragin, Early Far West Notebooks (1902-1923), Colorado Springs Pioneers Museum (typed transcription by Mrs. Shaw, 333-page PDF). Every mention of French speakers has been located and summarized; each entry refers to the PDF pages.
    • Research — French-speaking traces found in the literature and on the web (sources cited in each entry).
    • Hafen and Hafen MM — the sketches of French Fur Traders and Voyageurs in the American West (Hafen, ed., 1995), read in full and mapped step by step, and a survey of volumes 2 and 4 to 9 of The Mountain Men and the Fur Trade of the Far West: every dated place in the biographies of 68 French speakers, with the page (automatic identification, to be checked).
    • Tassé — Joseph Tassé, Les Canadiens de l'Ouest (Montreal, 1878), vols. I and II.
    • French Illinois, La francophonie nord-américaine (Atlas historique du Québec, 2012), Franco-Amérique (Louder and Waddell, 2008), New France (Margry, Johnson, Marchand), New England (Vermette, MacDonald, Belisle) and The Oregon Trail (Bergeron).
    • archive.org library (in progress) — public-domain books read in full text on archive.org, one corpus per book, each entry with the passage cited and a link to the book: hundreds of volumes, from Morice's Dictionnaire historique and Houck's History of Missouri to the Louisiana Historical Quarterly. The detailed list, with the number of entries per book, is in the French version (About).
    • Aubry Atlas — cross-references to the entries of the Geohistorical Atlas of François-Xavier Aubry (1824-1854): name, date, place and a one-sentence summary; the full entry, its sources and images remain in the Aubry Atlas.

    Reading the entries

    Dates are expressed in decimal years for filtering and in EDTF (~ approximate, ? uncertain). Certainty refers to the location: certain (place identified), approximate (place known, point estimated), hypothetical (identification or position to be checked). The descriptions are summaries written for the atlas and translated from the French; names are given in their French form when attested, with the notebooks' spelling in parentheses.

    Data: data/lieux.geojson (EPSG:4326), editable in QGIS; English translations in data/traductions-en.json.
