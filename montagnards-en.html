Hafen's series

From 1965 to 1972 LeRoy R. Hafen edited The Mountain Men and the Fur Trade of the Far West, ten volumes of biographies of the trappers, traders and guides of the American West, written by about a hundred historians. This page indexes the seven volumes gathered here (2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9): 229 biographies, 68 of them of French speakers (Canadians, St. Louis and Louisiana Creoles, French, Métis), and 7708 dated places found in their text, each with its page.

And Aubry? Hafen never names Aubry in these seven volumes: arriving in the West in 1846, he belonged to the Santa Fe trade rather than the fur trade. But a dozen of these men crossed his path or that of his circle (see below). Four also appear in Tassé: Antoine Leroux, Pierre Ménard, Joseph Robidoux, and “Old Pino” Fournaise, who is the Jacques Fournier of Tassé's volume II, who died at the Kansas City bluffs in 1871.

Method and limits

  • Automatic extraction. The scanned text of the seven volumes was split into biographies. Every sentence of the running text (notes and bibliographic references are left out) was matched against a gazetteer of 327 fur-trade places: posts, forts, rendezvous sites, rivers, passes, towns. One mention = one person, one place, one year, one page.
  • Years. The year is that of the sentence; failing that, the last year mentioned earlier in the biography (field ax = 0). Some mentions refer to a place named without the person having gone there.
  • Homonyms. “Fort William” means Fort Laramie, Bent's Fort or Fort William on Lake Superior depending on context; “Fort Union” the Missouri post or the New Mexico fort (after 1851). These cases are resolved by a simple rule and may contain errors.
  • Roles and dates. The roles summarized in the index were written for the atlas; Hafen's biography is authoritative.
  • Missing volumes. Volumes 1 (general introduction and first biographies), 3 and 10 are not included.
  • Data. hafen.geojson (7708 places) and hafen_personnes.json (the 229 biographies). Source: Hafen, LeRoy R. (dir.) (1965-1972), The Mountain Men and the Fur Trade of the Far West, Glendale, Arthur H. Clark, vol. 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 et 9.
