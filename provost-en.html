Étienne Provost c. 1785-1850

Français← Back to the French-Canadian atlas

Born in Chambly, the trapper Étienne Provost spent some forty years in the West: a prisoner of the Spaniards in New Mexico (1817), a pioneer of the Great Salt Lake basin (the city and river of Provo bear his name), then a trusted man of the American Fur Company on the upper Missouri. He died in St. Louis on July 3, 1850. This page gathers the atlas entries about him and the inquiry into his St. Louis years.

On the map

His route, after LeRoy R. Hafen's sketch (The Mountain Men, vol. 6, p. 371-386), can be followed on the Mountain Men page (“show” button). The 52 places were extracted automatically from the sketch: they are dated and paged but remain to be checked.

Other entries: Chouteau’s Island on the Arkansas (1816-1817) (Atlas historique du Québec); in St. Louis, the tavern and grocery, the Second Street Frenchtown, the corner of Second and Lombard, the “Henry N. Provost” of 1851 and his death and burial (July 3-4, 1850).

St. Louis: what we know

  • A Second Street shopkeeper. According to Jack B. Tykal, Provost kept a tavern and grocery in St. Louis from about 1827. Gilles Havard places him in Frenchtown, south of the Old Cathedral (Second and Third streets, between Poplar and Lombard), from the 1847 directory and the 1845 municipal census.
  • The Lamberts. Provost had married Marie-Rose Lambert; at his death, the cathedral register lists him as the husband of “Maria Rose Lombard.” In 1845 the directory places Clément Lambert's coffee-house at the northeast corner of Second and Lombard, facing an “H. N. Provost,” grocer, at the southwest corner.
  • But mind the first name. The 1851 directory, published after Étienne's death, still lists “Provost Henry N., mountain trader, 244 s Second.” The Provost of Second and Lombard was therefore probably another man, perhaps a relative.
  • Chambly. The neighbourhood was also that of Moïse Lamoureux, a native of Chambly like Provost (northwest corner of Second and Poplar in 1845).

The corner of Second and Lombard lay outside the area razed in 1939-1942 for the Gateway Arch grounds, which stopped at Poplar Street.

To be checked

  • Étienne's entry in the St. Louis Directory of 1847 (p. 153) and in the 1845 municipal census (Missouri Historical Society);
  • the Old Cathedral registers (baptisms, marriages, godparents), to see who surrounds the Provosts and the Lamberts;
  • Provost's probate inventory (estate file 3242, Missouri State Archives), which may name his debtors and creditors;
  • the identity of Henry N. Provost, “mountain trader” (1851).
